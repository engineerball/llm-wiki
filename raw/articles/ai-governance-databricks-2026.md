# AI Governance Best Practices: How to Build Responsible and Effective AI Programs

**Publisher:** Databricks Staff
**Source URL:** https://www.databricks.com/blog/ai-governance-best-practices-how-build-responsible-and-effective-ai-programs
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-10

## Evidence Summary

Databricks frames enterprise AI governance as management of risk, compliance, and trust as AI systems move into production and high-stakes use.

The article identifies data access controls, data lineage and observability, built-in safeguards for PII and unsafe content, and regulatory compliance as core governance pillars.

It recommends governance across the whole AI system, including data, prompts, workflows, human decision points, models, and downstream applications, rather than governing models in isolation.

The article emphasizes fairness and bias mitigation, transparency and explainability, accountable ownership, privacy and security, configurable safeguards, centralized access control, role-based access, audit trails, and shadow-AI reduction.

A practical framework should align control intensity with business impact and risk.
It should define cross-functional roles, RACI decision rights, human-in-the-loop requirements, risk classification, documentation, approval thresholds, monitoring, incident response, and audit expectations.

Lifecycle checkpoints should cover intended and prohibited use, data sources and constraints, evaluation criteria, release gates, rollback criteria, post-deployment monitoring, drift, unexpected inputs or outputs, and use outside the approved scope.

Recommended governance artifacts include system summaries, data documentation, evaluation summaries, monitoring plans, risk assessments, approval records, incident records, and change history.

Databricks recommends a centralized-federated operating model: a central function defines standards, policies, and risk frameworks while domain teams execute controls locally and remain accountable for outcomes.

The article recommends inventorying current AI use cases, classifying risk, assigning owners, piloting governance on high-impact systems, and expanding from those lessons.
