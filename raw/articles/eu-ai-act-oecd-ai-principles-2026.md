# EU AI Act and OECD AI Principles - governance reference notes

**EU source:** https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai
**OECD source:** https://oecd.ai/en/ai-principles
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-10

## EU AI Act

The European Commission describes Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 as a risk-based legal framework for AI providers and deployers.
The Commission groups AI systems into unacceptable, high, transparency, and minimal or no-risk categories.

The high-risk obligations described by the Commission include risk assessment and mitigation, dataset quality, activity logging and traceability, technical documentation, deployer information, human oversight, robustness, cybersecurity, and accuracy.

The Commission also describes transparency obligations for certain AI systems and obligations for providers of general-purpose AI models.
Organizations should assess applicability by role, use case, product context, territory, and applicable transition dates rather than treating the Act as a generic checklist.

## OECD AI Principles

The OECD AI Principles provide a values-based reference for trustworthy AI governance, including inclusive growth and sustainable development, human rights and democratic values, transparency and explainability, robustness/security/safety, and accountability.

## Interpretation Boundary

The EU AI Act is law with jurisdictional scope and enforcement consequences.
The OECD Principles are international policy principles, not a certification scheme.
Dates and implementation details must be checked against the current official legal text and Commission guidance before compliance decisions.
