# Celery Test

Diagnostic tool from [[Simon Sinek]] for testing whether an organization's WHY is genuinely clear. Part of the [[Golden Circle]] framework.

## The Scenario

You're in a supermarket with four options: M&Ms, rice milk, Oreos, and celery.

- Someone with **no clear WHY** might buy a mix — everything seems potentially useful or appealing.
- Someone with a clear WHY of *"I only consume what's healthy"* instantly buys only rice milk and celery. No deliberation.

The point: **a clear WHY makes decisions automatic.** You don't need a policy for every choice when the WHY is internalized.

## Organizational Application

Apply the test to any brand decision:

- A new partnership opportunity arrives. Should you take it?
- A product feature is proposed. Should you build it?
- A PR statement is needed. What tone?

If the organization's WHY is clear, every person in the room should reach the same answer instinctively. If people disagree about "which things fit the brand," the WHY is not clear enough.

## What It Reveals

| WHY Clarity | Decision Speed | Consistency |
|-------------|---------------|-------------|
| High | Fast, instinctive | High across team |
| Low | Slow, committee-driven | Low; depends on who's in the room |

When WHY is fuzzy, organizations compensate with process: approval chains, brand guidelines documents, committee reviews. These are symptoms, not solutions.

## Relationship to CDC

The Celery Test validates the "C" (Clarity) in the CDC framework ([[Golden Circle]]): Clarity of WHY, Discipline of HOW, Consistency of WHAT. Without Clarity, Discipline and Consistency cannot follow.
