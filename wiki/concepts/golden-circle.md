# Golden Circle

Framework by [[Simon Sinek]] explaining why some leaders and organizations inspire disproportionate loyalty and action. Three concentric rings, read inside-out:

```
        [ WHY  ]         ← innermost: purpose, cause, belief
      [ HOW    ]         ← middle: process, values, differentiators
    [ WHAT     ]         ← outermost: products, services, results
```

## The Core Insight

Every organization knows **WHAT** they do. Some know **HOW** they do it better. Very few can articulate **WHY** — and those few are the ones that inspire.

Most organizations communicate outside-in: *"We make computers. They're beautifully designed and easy to use. Want to buy one?"*

Inspiring organizations communicate inside-out: *"Everything we do challenges the status quo. We believe in thinking differently. We do this through beautifully designed, easy-to-use products. We also happen to make computers."*

Same information. Opposite order. The second version speaks to belief before products.

## Why It Works: Biology

The neocortex (newest brain layer) processes WHAT — rational analysis, language, features.
The limbic system (older) processes WHY — feelings, trust, loyalty, decision-making. It has no language center.

This is why people feel loyalty to a brand but struggle to articulate why. The decision was made in the limbic brain; the neocortex invents a post-hoc justification.

Communicating WHY first bypasses the rational filter and speaks directly to the decision-making system.

## The 3D Version: The Megaphone

In 3D, the Golden Circle is a cone (a megaphone). The WHY is the small end where the founder/leader stands. As the organization scales, the megaphone gets louder (more reach) but the message must stay clear. When the WHY gets diluted at scale, the organization loses the ability to inspire. See [[Walmart]].

## CDC: Operating Conditions

Three conditions required for the Golden Circle to work in practice:

1. **Clarity of WHY** — the purpose must be articulable and felt by everyone inside the organization, not just written on a wall
2. **Discipline of HOW** — values and processes must consistently express the WHY; every system, hiring decision, and partnership is filtered through it
3. **Consistency of WHAT** — every product, message, and action must be recognizable as coming from the same WHY

[[Southwest Airlines]] is the CDC example: WHY (champion of ordinary people), HOW (low cost, fun culture), WHAT (cheap domestic flights). Competitors copied the WHAT and HOW but lacked the WHY — customers felt the difference.

Test for CDC clarity: the [[Celery Test]]. If your team can't instantly agree on which "products" belong to the brand, the WHY is not clear enough.

## Applications

- **Marketing**: lead with belief, use product as proof
- **Hiring**: filter for people who believe what you believe, not just skills
- **Values**: write as verbs (actions), not nouns (qualities) — "Always do the right thing" beats "Integrity"
- **Leadership**: be a charisma leader (purpose-driven), not a power leader (incentive-driven)

## Examples

- [[Apple]] — canonical inside-out communicator; 2.5% market share, 100% thought leadership
- [[Harley-Davidson]] — logo tattoo = proof that beliefs > products
- [[Walmart]] — lost WHY after founder death; cautionary tale
- Martin Luther King Jr. — said "I have a dream," not "I have a plan"
- [[Southwest Airlines]] — CDC in action; WHY survived many low-cost imitators
- [[Samuel Pierpont Langley]] — WHAT-driven failure; had all resources, no WHY
