# Intent Thinking

**Intent Thinking** is the ability to translate business needs into **precise, testable descriptions of desired outcomes** for AI-driven software delivery.

In BCG Platinion's Agentic Software Factory framing, this is one of the two decisive competencies of the autonomous-delivery era, alongside [[harness-engineering]].

## Definition

Intent thinking is **not** mere prompt writing. It requires enough business, product, and technical understanding to specify:

- what the software should do
- what "correct" looks like
- which edge cases matter
- which trade-offs are acceptable
- what evidence should count as successful completion

A strong intent thinker does not only request an outcome. They define the boundaries within which that outcome is acceptable.

## Why It Matters

When coding throughput becomes abundant, the scarce resource shifts upstream. The bottleneck is no longer typing speed or even implementation detail. The bottleneck becomes the organization's ability to express:

- the right problem
- the right constraints
- the right acceptance criteria
- the right priorities

This makes intent quality a direct determinant of software quality.

## In the Delivery Lifecycle

Within the Agentic Software Factory model, intent thinking is most visible in:

- **Inception** — translating business goals into specifications and work packages
- **Stage gates** — deciding whether the produced outcome actually satisfies the original intent
- **Scenario design** — defining realistic behavioral tests that verify end-to-end correctness

## Relationship to Other Concepts

### Relationship to [[harness-engineering]]
Intent thinking defines **what** should be achieved. [[harness-engineering]] defines **how** the delivery system should guide and verify agents while achieving it.

### Relationship to [[ai-engineering-shift]]
As the [[ai-engineering-shift]] moves bottlenecks away from coding, intent thinking becomes a higher-value human skill.

### Relationship to [[institutional-ai]]
Like [[institutional-ai]], intent thinking assumes competitive advantage comes from domain understanding and organizational clarity, not just access to models.

## Practical Heuristic

If an agent completes a task but stakeholders still say "this isn't what we meant," the failure was often upstream in intent thinking rather than downstream in code generation.
