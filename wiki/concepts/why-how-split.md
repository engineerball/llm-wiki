# WHY-HOW Split

Structural pattern identified by [[Simon Sinek]]: great purpose-driven organizations almost always have a WHY visionary paired with a HOW operator. Neither role alone builds a durable, scalable organization.

## The Two Roles

**WHY Visionary**
- Source of belief, purpose, inspiration
- Communicates the cause; attracts believers
- Often impractical, impatient with process
- Thinks in possibilities and identity

**HOW Operator**
- Translates WHY into systems, process, structure
- Financial discipline, operational execution
- Builds the organization that carries the WHY at scale
- Thinks in constraints and execution

## Historical Examples

| WHY Visionary | HOW Operator | Organization |
|---|---|---|
| [[Walt Disney]] | Roy Disney | The Walt Disney Company |
| [[Steve Jobs]] | Steve Wozniak | [[Apple]] (early years) |
| Martin Luther King Jr. | Organizational infrastructure | Civil rights movement |

## Why Both Are Required

A WHY leader without a HOW partner generates inspiration but no durable structure. The organization runs on the founder's personality — fragile and unscalable.

A HOW operator without a WHY source builds efficient machinery with no soul. It optimizes WHAT metrics (cost, margin, throughput) and drifts toward [[Manipulation vs Inspiration|manipulation]].

The split also explains why replacing a WHY leader with a WHAT/HOW executive causes purpose decay. When [[Steve Jobs]] was ousted from [[Apple]], his HOW replacement defaulted to metrics. The WHY was not in the org chart — it was in Jobs.

## Scaling Implication

As organizations grow, the WHY-HOW split must be institutionalized — embedded into culture, hiring, and values — rather than residing solely in two founders. Otherwise it's still fragile: lose either person and the balance breaks.
