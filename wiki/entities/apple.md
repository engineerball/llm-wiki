# Apple

Technology company. Founded by Steve Jobs and Steve Wozniak (Cupertino, CA). Primary example in [[Simon Sinek]]'s [[Golden Circle]] framework as a WHY-first organization.

## WHY-First Model

Apple's stated WHY: *empower individuals to challenge the status quo.* This manifests consistently across products, packaging, advertising, and store design — not just marketing copy.

Their 1984 Macintosh ad was not a product announcement. It was an ideology declaration: one person resisting totalitarian control.

The `i` prefix (iMac, iPhone, iPad) signals individual empowerment — not just a naming convention.

## Relevance to [[Purpose-Driven Leadership]]

- Only ~2.5% market share in PC (innovation adopters), yet unquestioned thought leader
- Able to enter new categories (music, phones, watches) and be taken seriously immediately
- Customers who share Apple's beliefs become loyal even against objectively competitive alternatives
- Employees defend Apple's quality because they believe in the mission, not just the paycheck

## The Jobs Ouster — WHY Fragility

When [[Steve Jobs]] was forced out of Apple in 1985, Apple retained the same WHAT (computers) and HOW (premium design) but lost the WHY. Under new leadership, Apple optimized for product roadmaps and market share metrics — WHAT-first thinking.

The company nearly went bankrupt. Jobs returned in 1997, immediately launched the "Think Different" campaign, and restored the WHY. The subsequent product run (iMac, iPod, iPhone, iPad) is direct evidence that WHY — not technology or design talent — was the source of Apple's differentiation.

Key lesson: WHY is not a product strategy. It's an organizational identity. If it lives only in one person, the organization is one departure away from losing it.
