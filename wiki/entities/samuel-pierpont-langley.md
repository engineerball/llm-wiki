# Samuel Pierpont Langley

American aviation pioneer (1834–1906). Used by [[Simon Sinek]] as the counterexample to the Wright Brothers — a WHAT-driven leader who had every structural advantage and still lost.

## The Setup

When the airplane race began, most Americans expected Langley to win:
- Professor at Smithsonian Institution; respected scientist
- Funded by the US War Department ($50,000 — enormous sum at the time)
- Had talented, well-paid team assembled
- Had prior research and prestige

The Wright Brothers had none of this: no funding, no formal engineering credentials, no team beyond themselves.

## Why He Lost

Langley's WHY was fame and legacy — the WHAT of being "first." His team worked for pay, not belief. When they failed repeatedly, there was no shared deeper purpose to sustain them.

The Wright Brothers had WHY: they believed flight could fundamentally change the world. This belief sustained them through repeated crashes. Their team worked for the mission, not a paycheck.

## The Telling Detail

When news of the Wright Brothers' success reached Langley, he immediately abandoned his project — despite having substantial resources remaining. A person driven by WHY would have continued, wanting to contribute to flight regardless of who was first. Langley stopped because his goal was the prize, not the purpose.

## Contrast

| | Wright Brothers | Langley |
|---|---|---|
| Resources | None | Ample |
| Team | Family | Paid experts |
| Driver | WHY (change humanity) | WHAT (fame) |
| After losing | Kept innovating | Quit immediately |
