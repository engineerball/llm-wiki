# Steve Jobs

Co-founder of [[Apple]] (with Steve Wozniak). Primary WHY visionary behind Apple's identity as a challenger of the status quo. Used by [[Simon Sinek]] to illustrate both WHY-driven leadership and what happens when a WHY leader is removed from their organization.

## WHY Visionary

Jobs embodied Apple's WHY: *empower individuals to challenge the status quo, think differently.* Every product decision, marketing campaign, and design choice flowed from this belief.

The 1984 Macintosh ad and the "Think Different" campaign both reflect Jobs' personal WHY more than any product strategy.

## The Ouster — WHY Decay

When Apple's board pushed Jobs out in 1985, the company didn't just lose a CEO. It lost the source of its WHY.

Under subsequent leadership, Apple defaulted to WHAT-first thinking — product roadmaps, market share, revenue metrics. The company still made computers but lost the ability to inspire. Sales declined; Apple nearly went bankrupt.

## The Return — WHY Restored

Jobs returned in 1997. He immediately reinstated the WHY: "Think Different" campaign launched within a year. Products like the iMac, iPod, and eventually iPhone followed — not because Jobs invented new technology, but because every product was filtered through the same original WHY.

This is Sinek's clearest proof that WHY is not a product strategy or a CEO style. It's an organizational identity that must be consciously maintained.

## Visionary–Operator Pattern

Jobs (WHY) needed Steve Wozniak (HOW/engineering). See [[Walt Disney]] for the same pattern. Great WHY leaders are often impractical without a HOW partner. See [[Why-HOW Split]].
