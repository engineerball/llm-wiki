# Walmart

American retail giant. Founded by Sam Walton in 1962. Used by [[Simon Sinek]] as a cautionary tale for purpose decay.

## The Rise

Sam Walton's WHY was not low prices — that was the HOW. His actual WHY: *build an organization for everyone* — serve ordinary people the way wealthy people are served, and treat employees with corresponding respect. Employee care was structural to the WHY, not an HR policy add-on. That belief drove loyalty from employees and customers beyond rational explanation.

Walton competed against Kmart and Target, both founded the same year, both with more capital and better locations. Yet Walmart became dominant. Low prices alone don't explain it.

## The Decline

After Sam Walton's death, leadership failed to articulate the WHY. The company kept the same WHAT (retail) and HOW (low prices) but the WHY became invisible. Critically, the new focus on "lowest prices" dropped the employee-care dimension — Walmart began treating employees as cost centers rather than as part of the mission.

Employees and customers felt the shift. Public perception turned negative. The company that communities once loved became a symbol of corporate harm.

Kmart and Target did the same things — low prices, mass retail — yet only Walmart attracted strong feelings in both directions. That's proof the loyalty was never about price.

The second Thai summary adds nuance: Walmart's decay is also a metric drift story. Leadership began measuring only WHAT metrics (revenue, margins, store count). Without WHY metrics (employee satisfaction, community trust, customer loyalty quality), the organization had no signal that its purpose was eroding.

## Lesson

Purpose stored only in a founder's head is a liability. Organizations must institutionalize WHY into systems, culture, and hiring before they scale. See [[Purpose-Driven Leadership]].
