# What is FinOps, and Why Do We Need It?

**Source:** Atlassian Blog
**Date:** Originally published circa 2020-2021 (cloud migration surge context)

## Summary

Atlassian's introductory guide to FinOps (cloud financial management), covering the definition, rationale, five best practices, and the three-phase lifecycle.

## What is FinOps?

FinOps is the practice of bringing financial accountability to the variable spend model of cloud, enabling distributed teams to make business trade-offs between speed, cost, and quality. It's about **optimizing cloud spend to get the most value** — not just reducing costs. Key activities include:

- Negotiating rates with vendors
- Tracking and optimizing free trials
- Auditing licenses and use
- Forecasting costs

## Why Do We Need It?

Cloud migration can yield ~20% average savings, but those savings are **not guaranteed**. Companies without strong FinOps operations end up paying for:

- Things they don't need
- Tools no one is using
- Abandoned licenses

Without ongoing optimization, cloud spending can balloon over time despite the inherent cost advantages of cloud.

## 5 FinOps Best Practices

### 1. Plan for FinOps Before You Migrate
Best time to think about FinOps is **before** migration. Plan for cost optimization in scaling and shadow IT early. Cloud billing is fundamentally different from on-prem: you can purchase exactly what you need now and scale up/down. Automate license renewals/expirations aligned with business needs (e.g., contractor licenses expire at contract end).

### 2. Don't Sacrifice Value for Savings
FinOps is **not** about finding the cheapest option — it's about balancing savings with their trade-offs. Example: a three-month free trial saves money, but paid plans may offer features/support/integrations that provide more value depending on context.

### 3. Calculate Your Actual Costs
Understanding what you're spending (TCO, ROI) is harder than expected, especially in large orgs with siloed teams. Key responsibilities:
- Calculating current TCO and ROI
- Forecasting future costs
- Doing regular audits

### 4. Build FinOps as an Ongoing Practice
Not a one-time task. Most successful companies set **clear, ongoing roles and responsibilities** for long-term cost control. Include regular audits, forecasting, and FinOps team involvement in major software decisions.

### 5. Set Clear Responsibilities
Who owns FinOps? Dedicated professional? Embedded in existing teams? What are exact responsibilities? IT teams that shift from server maintenance to cloud have newly freed-up capacity — great candidates for FinOps roles. **If nobody owns it, it goes by the wayside.**

## Inform, Optimize, Operate

The three-phase FinOps lifecycle:

1. **Inform** — Get visibility into all cloud spending (licenses, maintenance, IT time); benchmark against internal goals and industry norms
2. **Optimize** — Ditch unneeded services/upgrades, automate, rightsizing resources, strategically analyze cloud spend
3. **Operate** — Continuous improvement for both cost reduction and innovation/efficiency enablement

**Key insight:** Top-performing teams don't stay in the operate phase — they regularly cycle back to inform and optimize.
