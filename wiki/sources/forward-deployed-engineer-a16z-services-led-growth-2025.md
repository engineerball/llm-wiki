# Trading Margin for Moat: Why the Forward Deployed Engineer Is the Hottest Job in Startups

**Source:** Andreessen Horowitz (a16z)  
**URL:** https://a16z.com/services-led-growth/

## Main Thesis

This article explains the rise of forward deployed engineers from a **go-to-market and company-building** perspective.
Its central claim is that complex AI products often require hands-on implementation work, and the companies willing to trade short-term margin for successful deployment can build durable moats.

## Why FDEs Matter in AI

a16z's framing is simple:
AI is easy to demo and hard to deploy inside real enterprise systems.
Customers need help integrating models with internal databases, APIs, workflows, and context.
That implementation burden is exactly where forward deployed teams matter.

The article argues that these teams operationalize the model into a real-world solution.

## Services-Led Growth

The article compares AI startups to earlier enterprise platforms like Salesforce, ServiceNow, and Workday.
Those businesses became dominant partly because they did difficult implementation work early, even at the cost of lower gross margins.

The implication for FDEs:

- they are not a sidecar,
- they are part of the wedge,
- and they help companies become the system of work or system of record.

## What Forward Deployed Teams Actually Do

In the article, forward deployed teams:

- customize AI products for a customer's exact environment,
- stand up integrations,
- redesign workflows around agentic systems,
- and help onboard AI the way managers onboard human employees.

This framing broadens the role beyond just engineering implementation.
The FDE becomes part engineer, part workflow re-designer, part operationalizer of digital labor.

## Best Practices for Building Such Teams

The article is valuable because it also describes organizational patterns:

1. pick customers and scope carefully,
2. align incentives with account success,
3. build reusable internal libraries and integration surfaces,
4. document everything,
5. automate services work aggressively,
6. hire curious, high-agency people,
7. create strong feedback loops between front line and product,
8. spend time in person with customers.

These are effectively design principles for FDE-led organizations.

## Strategic Insight

One of the strongest ideas in the article is that forward deployed teams are not merely a cost center.
They are a **moat-building mechanism** during platform shifts.
The work they do can later be standardized, automated, and absorbed into the product, but someone has to pioneer that work first.
